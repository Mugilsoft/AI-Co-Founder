# Global AI Framework Comparison

**Mugil Soft Solutions — Portfolio Asset | Week 3**

> A comparative analysis of four major global AI governance frameworks. This document helps organisations understand the landscape of AI ethics, principles, risk management, and regulation — and what each framework requires.

---

## Important Distinctions

Before comparing frameworks, it is essential to understand four distinct categories:

| Category | Purpose | Example |
|----------|---------|---------|
| **Ethics** | Moral principles that guide what is right and wrong in AI development and use | "AI should not be used to manipulate vulnerable people" |
| **Principles** | High-level commitments that organisations or governments adopt to guide AI policy | "AI systems should be transparent and explainable" |
| **Risk Management** | Structured processes for identifying, assessing, and mitigating AI-related risks | "Conduct bias testing before deploying an AI hiring system" |
| **Legal Obligations** | Binding laws and regulations that carry enforcement and penalties | "High-risk AI systems must undergo conformity assessment before market placement" |

These categories are not mutually exclusive — a legal framework may incorporate ethical principles — but they carry different levels of obligation and consequence.

## Detailed Framework Analysis

### 1. UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021)

**Type:** Ethical recommendation (non-binding)

**Scope:** Global — adopted by all 193 UNESCO member states

**Core Values:**
- Human rights and human dignity
- Living in peaceful, just, and interconnected societies
- Ensuring diversity and inclusiveness
- Environment and ecosystem flourishing
- Proportionality and do no harm
- Safety and security
- Fairness and non-discrimination
- Sustainability
- Right to privacy and data protection
- Transparency and explainability
- Human oversight and determination
- Responsibility and accountability
- Awareness and literacy
- Multi-stakeholder and adaptive governance

**Key Policy Areas:**
- Data governance
- Education and research
- Communication and information
- Economy and labour
- Health and social well-being
- Environment

**Strengths:**
- Broadest international consensus (193 countries)
- Covers environmental and social dimensions often missing from other frameworks
- Emphasises human rights perspective

**Limitations:**
- Non-binding — no enforcement mechanism
- Very broad — difficult to operationalise directly
- Limited practical implementation guidance

**Relevance for Mugil Soft Solutions:** Useful as an ethical foundation and for demonstrating alignment with international norms — particularly for projects involving diverse populations or public sector clients.

---

### 2. OECD AI Principles (2019, updated 2024)

**Type:** Intergovernmental principles (non-binding, but influential)

**Scope:** 38 OECD member countries plus partner nations

**Core Principles:**
1. **Inclusive growth, sustainable development, and well-being** — AI should benefit people and the planet
2. **Human-centred values and fairness** — AI should respect human rights, democratic values, and diversity
3. **Transparency and explainability** — AI actors should provide meaningful information about AI systems
4. **Robustness, security, and safety** — AI systems should function appropriately and not pose unreasonable risks
5. **Accountability** — Organisations and individuals developing and deploying AI should be accountable

**Recommendations for Governments:**
- Facilitate public and private investment in AI R&D
- Foster a digital ecosystem for AI
- Shape an enabling policy environment
- Build human capacity and prepare for labour market transformation
- Cooperate internationally for trustworthy AI

**Strengths:**
- High international credibility and political influence
- Regularly updated (2024 revision reflects recent AI developments)
- Referenced by many national AI strategies
- OECD.AI Policy Observatory provides practical tools

**Limitations:**
- Non-binding
- Principles-level — requires interpretation for implementation
- Primarily government-facing, less directly actionable for companies

**Relevance for Mugil Soft Solutions:** Useful for client-facing materials, government project proposals, and demonstrating alignment with internationally recognised principles.

### 3. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, 2023)

**Type:** Voluntary risk management framework (non-binding)

**Scope:** US-originated, internationally applicable

**Core Functions:**

| Function | Purpose | Key Activities |
|----------|---------|----------------|
| **GOVERN** | Establish and maintain AI governance structures | Policies, roles, accountability, culture, legal compliance |
| **MAP** | Understand the AI system's context and risks | Context analysis, stakeholder identification, risk identification, impact characterisation |
| **MEASURE** | Assess identified risks using quantitative and qualitative methods | Bias testing, performance evaluation, security assessment, reliability measurement |
| **MANAGE** | Prioritise and act on risks based on assessment | Risk treatment, resource allocation, response planning, monitoring, communication |

**Key Characteristics:**
- Risk-based and outcome-focused
- Lifecycle approach (design → development → deployment → monitoring → decommission)
- Includes Trustworthy AI Characteristics: valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, fair with harmful bias managed
- AI RMF Playbook provides suggested actions for each subcategory
- Companion documents: AI RMF Generative AI Profile, AI RMF Crosswalk

**Strengths:**
- Most practically actionable framework available
- Flexible — adaptable to any organisation size or AI use case
- Well-structured with clear methodology
- Regularly updated with companion resources
- Voluntary nature reduces compliance burden while providing rigour

**Limitations:**
- Voluntary — no enforcement mechanism
- Requires organisational effort to implement effectively
- US-centric origin (though broadly applicable)
- Does not prescribe specific technical standards

**Relevance for Mugil Soft Solutions:** Primary operational framework for AI risk management. The Govern-Map-Measure-Manage structure directly maps to Mugil's AI Governance Gate and project governance processes.

### 4. EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

**Type:** Legally binding regulation (enforceable law)

**Scope:** European Union — applies to AI systems placed on the EU market, regardless of where the provider is based

**Risk Classification:**

| Category | Description | Examples | Requirements |
|----------|-------------|----------|-------------|
| **Unacceptable Risk** | AI practices banned in the EU | Social scoring by governments, real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with limited exceptions), manipulation of vulnerable groups, emotion recognition in workplaces/schools | **Prohibited** |
| **High Risk** | AI systems in critical areas that significantly affect people's lives | AI in hiring/recruitment, credit scoring, education assessment, law enforcement, migration, critical infrastructure, medical devices | Conformity assessment, technical documentation, quality management, data governance, human oversight, accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity, registration, post-market monitoring |
| **Limited Risk** | AI systems with specific transparency obligations | Chatbots, deepfakes, AI-generated content | Disclosure that content is AI-generated or that the user is interacting with an AI |
| **Minimal Risk** | AI systems with no additional requirements | Spam filters, AI in video games | No additional requirements (existing laws apply) |

**Key Obligations for High-Risk AI:**
1. Risk management system throughout the AI lifecycle
2. Data governance for training, validation, and testing data
3. Technical documentation
4. Record-keeping (logging)
5. Transparency and information provision
6. Human oversight measures
7. Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity
8. Quality management system
9. Conformity assessment (self-assessment or third-party)
10. EU declaration of conformity
11. CE marking
12. Registration in EU database
13. Post-market monitoring
14. Serious incident reporting

**Penalties:**
- Prohibited AI practices: up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover
- Other violations: up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover
- Incorrect information to authorities: up to €7.5 million or 1% of global annual turnover

**Timeline:**
- February 2025: Prohibitions on unacceptable-risk AI take effect
- August 2025: Provisions on general-purpose AI models apply
- August 2026: Most obligations for high-risk AI systems apply

**Strengths:**
- First comprehensive AI law globally — sets a global precedent
- Clear risk classification provides certainty
- Enforceable with significant penalties
- Covers the full AI lifecycle

**Limitations:**
- Complex compliance requirements
- Significant burden for SMEs (though some provisions are lighter)
- Interpretation of some categories still evolving
- Only directly applicable in the EU (though has extraterritorial reach)

**Relevance for Mugil Soft Solutions:** Essential for any project where the AI system will be deployed in or offered to the EU market. Also serves as a benchmark for high-risk AI governance practices globally. Understanding EU AI Act requirements positions Mugil to advise clients on compliance.
